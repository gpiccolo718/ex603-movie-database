# Unit 3 Finding: The Dashboard Failure Rate

To the dashboard team: two weeks ago, your dashboard reported that about half of all ratings on the platform end in failure, and leadership froze the quarterly payout based on that number. I checked the figure against the underlying data, and it is not correct. Out of 200 ratings, 156 (78%) were published normally, 34 (17%) were withdrawn, and 10 (5%) were flagged for review. Counting only withdrawn ratings as failures, the real failure rate is about 1 in 6. Even if flagged ratings are counted as failures too, the rate is about 1 in 5. Neither is close to half, which matches the doubts raised by the colleague who questioned the result.

The error comes from how the data handles empty fields. Every rating has a field for the reason it was withdrawn, but that field is only filled in when a rating actually is withdrawn. For the large majority of ratings, which were successful, it is left blank. When the data is filtered on a field like this, the system quietly skips the blank rows instead of counting them. It does not show an error or a warning, so the report looks complete. It is a bit like running a survey and silently throwing out everyone who left one question blank, then reporting the answers of whoever is left. In my own testing, a filter of this kind dropped 166 of the 200 ratings without any warning. Because the ratings left out were mostly successful ones, the failures made up a much larger share of what remained, which inflates the failure rate. I have not reviewed the dashboard's own calculation directly, but this is the same type of error, and it explains why the result looked reasonable while being wrong.

I recommend that leadership revisit the payout freeze using the corrected failure rate of about 17%, since the decision was based on a figure roughly three times too high. Before the dashboard is used for any further decisions, it should be corrected so that ratings with blank fields are counted rather than silently dropped, and its results should be checked against a simple total of all ratings. I also suggest reviewing any other dashboards built in the same way, because this kind of error produces no warning and can easily go unnoticed. I am happy to help verify the corrected numbers.
