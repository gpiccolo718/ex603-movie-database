# Unit 5 Finding: Two Different Problems in the Catalog

**To the leadership team**

My earlier report named three movies with unusually high withdrawal rates. Connecting the ratings to our users, movies, and genres shows that the catalog actually has **two separate problems**: some movies get attention but attract bad ratings, and some movies get no attention at all. They have different causes, so they need different responses.

## Group 1: Movies that are rated often, but whose ratings are frequently withdrawn

| Movie | Ratings | Withdrawn | Main reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Movie 11 | 16 | 5 (31%) | Mixed: 2 removed by the user, plus spam, a policy violation, and a duplicate |
| Movie 9 | 14 | 4 (29%) | Mixed: 2 removed by the user, plus spam and a policy violation |
| Movie 2 | 16 | 4 (25%) | Spam: 3 of the 4 |

The typical rate for movies with plenty of ratings is about 16%. These movies are not unpopular. People are rating them. The problem is the quality of those ratings. Comparing each one with other movies from the same country showed it has a higher rate than all of them, so the cause appears to be specific to each movie, not to where it comes from.

**Recommended action: review the ratings, not the movies.**

- **Movie 2:** Treat it as a likely spam target. Have the moderation team review the accounts that submitted its spam ratings and tighten spam checks on this movie. The movie itself does not need to be removed.
- **Movies 11 and 9:** Their withdrawals have several causes, and half were withdrawn by the users themselves. Before acting, a moderator should read the withdrawn ratings to find out why users are pulling them back. Movie 9 has only 14 ratings, so its rate could change quickly. Keep it under watch rather than taking action now.

## Group 2: Movies that have never been rated

**Movies 14 and 15** have received **no ratings at all**, even though both are listed as available and have been in the catalog since early 2022. Our standard reports leave them out completely, because they only show movies with at least one rating. That made the catalog look fully active when about 1 in 8 movies had no engagement.

This is not a ratings-quality problem. Nothing has gone wrong with these movies' ratings, because there are none. The likely issue is that **users are not finding them**. Neither movie has a genre tag, so neither appears when users browse by genre. The data cannot confirm this is the cause: one other movie also has no genre tags and still gets rated.

**Recommended action: make them findable, then decide.**

1. Add genre tags to both movies and feature them for a limited period.
2. Check again after that period. If they start getting ratings, the problem was visibility. If they still get none, review whether they are worth keeping in the catalog.
3. Change our engagement reports so every movie appears, including those with zero ratings, so this kind of gap cannot stay hidden again.

## What we know and what we don't

The data shows which movies have high withdrawal rates, why each rating was withdrawn, and which movies have never been rated. It does not show **why** users withdraw their own ratings or **why** nobody rates movies 14 and 15. Answering those questions would need information we do not currently store, such as viewing activity, search and browsing behavior, or feedback from users.
